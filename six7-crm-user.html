1. Getting started#

1.1 What the CRM is for#

The CRM keeps your customers and your sales in one place. Companies are the accounts you sell to, contacts are the people there, and deals are what you're trying to sell them. Each deal moves through stages, from Lead to Won or Lost, and every call, email, meeting and task is logged against it. Each morning, My day tells you what to do first.

Why Six7, and how its apps work, is in the Suite manual.

1.2 Opening the app#

Open the link you were given and bookmark it. Google signs you in: there's no separate password. Use the right Google account, as in Suite §5.

If the app says "Sign in with your work account" or "You don't have access to Six7 CRM yet", see Suite §5.

1.3 Your role#

Solo

The CRM is yours alone. You're its Admin and you own every record, so everything in this manual is open to you. Sections about other people are marked Team: skip them.

Team

Your role is shown at the top right, under your name, with your region:

  • Sales Rep: you see the companies you own and everything under them, and the deals and contacts you own.
  • Operations Manager: you see your region, or everything if you have none, and look after its data.
  • Sales Manager: you see everything.
  • Admin: you see everything and run the app.

Each task below says who can do it. The general model is in Suite §6.

2. A tour of the app#

The bar at the top has My day, Pipeline, Deals, Contacts, Companies and Reports. On the right: + New (a deal, contact, company or activity), a search box, a refresh button, and your initials, which open Your settings. Managers and Admins also see Admin tools.

2.1 My day, the home screen#

What needs you today, in five counts and their lists:

  • Overdue tasks, the oldest first;
  • Today: tasks, calls and meetings, by time;
  • Follow-ups due: deals whose next step date has come;
  • Stale deals: open deals with no activity, stage change or edit for more than 14 days;
  • Update close date: open deals whose expected close date has passed.

Each row has its quick actions: Done, Log activity, Open, Move to tomorrow for an overdue task, and Add to Calendar or Log notes for a meeting. At the top: Log activity, Add task and New deal.

Team Managers can switch My day between Mine, All and one person.

2.2 Pipeline#

Your open deals as a board: one column per stage, with the count and total value, then Won and Lost for this quarter. Each card shows the deal's name, value, expected close date and owner, its next step, and badges such as Stale and Follow-up due.

Filter by Customer, Stage and Expected close. Saved filters keeps the filters you use often, in your browser. Copy link shares the board with its filters.

2.3 Deals#

The Deals list shows open deals by default; switch it to won, lost or all. Click a deal to open its page:

  • the stage bar at the top, which you can click to change the stage;
  • the value, probability and weighted value, expected close and its quarter, owner and source;
  • the Next step, with Change;
  • a box to log a call, email, meeting, note or task, and the timeline below it;
  • the primary contact, with Email in Gmail, Log email, Call and Log call;
  • the company, its other open deals and its contacts;
  • Edit, History, Copy link and Archive.

2.4 Contacts and Companies#

  • Contacts lists people with their job title, company, email and phone. A contact's panel shows their details, their deals and their timeline, with Email in Gmail and Add to Calendar.
  • Companies lists accounts with their domain, status, industry, open deals and account owner. A company's panel shows its contacts, its deals and the timeline of everything under it. Pipeline per quarter opens the reports for that customer only, and Board its deals on the Pipeline.

3. Everyday tasks#

3.1 Add a contact and their company#

  1. Click + New › Contact, or New contact on the Contacts screen.
  2. Type their First name, and at least one of Email, Phone or Mobile.
  3. In Company, start typing. Pick the company, or click + Create company "…" to create it in the same step.
  4. Click Save contact.

The app warns you when a company with the same name or domain, or a contact with the same email, already exists. If you can see it, choose Open existing or Save anyway. Team If someone else owns it, the warning says who: ask them or an Operations Manager to give you access.

3.2 Create a deal#

  1. Click New deal, on My day, the Pipeline or a company.
  2. Choose the Company, and the primary contact if you know them.
  3. Name the deal, for example Globex Retail — store support pilot.
  4. Set its Stage, Value and Expected close date. The expected close date decides the quarter it counts in.
  5. Save. The deal is yours.

The probability follows the stage, and you can change it.

3.3 Move a deal through the stages#

  • On the Pipeline: drag the card to its new stage. On a phone, use the card's Move to….
  • On the deal's page: click a stage on the stage bar.

Moving to Lost asks Why was it lost?. Choose a reason and click Mark as Lost; Cancel puts the card back. Moving to Won or Lost records today as the close date. A deal moved back out of Won or Lost is open again.

3.4 Log a call, email, meeting or note#

On a deal's page, use the box above the timeline:

  1. Choose Call, Email, Meeting, Note or Task. Call is chosen already.
  2. Type what it was about, and change the date and time if needed.
  3. Add notes if you want.
  4. Tick Set the next step to update the deal's next step at the same time.
  5. Click Log call (or the button for what you chose).

On a contact or a company, click Log activity (or Add task) for the same form. Anywhere else, Log activity on My day or + New › Activity does the same. An activity on a deal counts as activity: the deal is no longer stale.

3.5 Tasks and next steps#

  • Add task creates a task with a due date, linked to a deal, contact or company.
  • Done marks it done. On a deal, the app then offers to set the deal's next step.
  • Move to tomorrow on an overdue task moves its date.
  • A deal's Next step and its date show on its card. When the date comes, the deal appears in Follow-ups due.

3.6 Quick edit on the board#

Click the pencil on a Pipeline card to change its value, expected close date or next step without opening it.

3.7 Keep deals fresh#

  • Stale means no activity, stage change or edit for more than 14 days. Log what happened, or close the deal.
  • Update close date means the expected close date has passed. Change it on the deal, so the pipeline counts it in the right quarter.

3.8 Archive and restore#

Nothing in the CRM is deleted.

  • Archive: a record's owner, or a manager, clicks Archive on its page or panel. It leaves the lists, the board and search.
  • Team Restore: Operations Managers, Sales Managers and Admins tick Archived on a list to see archived records, open one and click Restore.

4. Working with others Team#

  • Who sees what follows owners. A company's owner sees everything under it, and so do the managers of its region. If you own a deal under someone else's company, you see only that company's name, domain, industry and country.
  • Duplicates owned by others: the app tells you the record exists and who owns it, and nothing more. Ask them or an Operations Manager for access.
  • Copy link on a record, the Pipeline or a report copies its address, with its filters. Whoever opens it sees the same view, limited to what their role can see.
  • Reassigning: when someone leaves, a manager moves their companies, contacts, open deals and open tasks to someone else in one step.

5. Finding things#

  • Search, at the top: part of a company's name or domain, or a contact's name or email, or a deal's name. It searches everything you can see.
  • List filters: on Deals, Show (open, won, lost or all), customer, stage and quarter; on Contacts, company and tag; on Companies, status, industry and tag. Managers also filter by owner.
  • Saved filters on the Pipeline and the Deals list keep the filters you use often.
  • Export to Sheets on a list makes a Google Sheet of what it shows, and opens Google's Make a copy page to save it in your own Drive.

6. Reports#

Reports shows five reports, each with a chart, a table and a sentence that sums it up. Choose Quarters back, Quarters ahead, Customer and Stage at the top. Team Managers also have Owner, and the Sales Manager, Admins and Operations Managers without a region also have Region.

ReportAnswers
Pipeline evolution by quarterHow big was the open pipeline at each quarter end, and how did it change?
Pipeline per quarterHow much is expected to close in each quarter ahead, by stage? Switch between the value and the weighted value
Won and lost by quarterWhat did we win and lose, our win rate, and why we lost
Pipeline by ownerEach person's open and weighted pipeline, stale deals and passed close dates
Pipeline by regionThe same per region

On Pipeline per quarter, click a bar segment to list the deals in it. Each report has Export to Sheets, Generate report (Doc) and Download PDF. At the top, Generate full report (Doc) and Download full PDF make one document with all five, and Copy link shares the report with its filters.

The scope line says what's counted: Team "Your records" for a Sales Rep, "Whole organisation" for the Sales Manager. Amounts are in the company's currency; deals in other currencies are totalled separately.

7. For managers Team#

Operations Managers (for their region) and Sales Managers can:

  • switch My day between Mine, All and one person;
  • filter the Pipeline, lists and reports by Owner (an Operations Manager sees the people of their region) and, without a region of your own, by Region;
  • change a record's owner, and use Admin tools › Reassign owner to move one person's work to another;
  • run Validate data and Import pasted rows (Operations Managers and Admins);
  • get the pipeline overview email, daily or weekly: the open pipeline and how it changed, what was won and lost, the pipeline charts, and what needs your attention (stale deals, passed close dates, the largest open deals, and deals lost or moved forward).

8. Email and notifications#

The daily digest arrives around 07:00 on days when you have something: tasks overdue or due today, today's meetings, follow-ups due, stale deals and close dates passed. Every item links to the record.

Your Admin turns the digest on or off for you. Your settings (your initials, top right) holds your Theme: System, Light or Dark, kept on this device.

9. Using it with Gmail and Calendar#

The CRM works with your own Gmail and Google Calendar, with nothing to install and no extra permission:

  • Email in Gmail on a contact opens a new Gmail message to them, in your work account. Afterwards, Log email records it in one click.
  • Call dials the contact's number on a phone; Log call records it.
  • Add to Calendar on a meeting, or a task with a time, opens a Google Calendar event with the time, the details and the contact as a guest. You save it in your own calendar.

10. Using it on a phone#

The CRM works in your phone's browser; there's no app to install.

  • My day, the lists and the records fit the screen.
  • On the Pipeline, move a deal with its Move to… button.
  • Call opens your phone's dialler.
  • Open links from CRM emails in your browser, not inside the Gmail app.

11. When something goes wrong#

What you seeWhat it means
A duplicate warning when you saveA record with the same name, domain or email exists. Open it, or save anyway
"… already exists and is owned by …" TeamSomeone else owns it. Ask them or an Operations Manager for access
You can't see an account you work on TeamIts company is owned by someone else. Ask an Operations Manager to reassign it, or to make you the owner of the deal
"The app is busy, please try again"Many changes at once. Try again in a moment
A deal sits in the wrong quarterCheck its expected close date (open deals) or close date (won deals)
A report copy is missingClick Make a copy on Google's page when the report opens, or generate it again
A link says the record isn't foundIt's archived, or you can't see it
"Six7 CRM is parked"The CRM is stopped for now, and nothing is lost. Team Ask your CRM Admin when it will be back
"Six7 CRM was retired on …"The CRM is closed for good, and nothing it held is lost. Solo It's all in the folder Six7 CRM — Retired followed by the date, in your Drive. Team Ask your CRM Admin where to find it
"Six7 CRM was removed on …"The CRM has been removed. Solo Its files are in your Drive's trash for 30 days. Team Ask your CRM Admin

Who to ask: Solo Six7 (Suite §10). Team Your CRM Admin, or your Operations Manager.
