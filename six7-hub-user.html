Hub

User manual

The Hub is your company's page for its Six7 apps. It lists them, sends your questions to Six7's support team, and keeps a record of what you asked. Chapter 6 explains the files your apps add to your Drive on their own.

Applies to the Six7 Hub 0.1.0 (alpha). Screens show sample data.

1. What the Hub is#

The Hub is a Six7 app like the others: a Google Sheet with a web app, in your own Google account. It has three screens:

  • My apps lists your company's Six7 apps, with a light for each one.
  • Ask Six7 for support sends one email to Six7's support team.
  • Requests keeps a record of what was sent.

Admins also have Admin tools.

1.1 Who can open it#

Team

Everyone in your company's Google Workspace domain can open the Hub as a Member, unless an Admin turns that off (§5.4). The Owner account is always an Admin.

Solo

In a solo company, your own account is the Owner account. The Hub is yours alone: you're its Admin, and nobody else can open it.

RoleWhat they can do
AdminSee every app, hidden ones too. Refresh list, Hide and List it again, Check installation. See every request. Ask Six7 for support
MemberSee the apps Admins haven't hidden. See their own requests. Ask Six7 for support when Admins allow it (§3)

1.2 Opening the Hub#

Open the Hub's web address, signed in with your work account. Keep it one click away, as the apps: bookmark it, or install it as an app on your computer or phone (Suite manual: Keep the apps one click away). On Google Workspace it takes the Hub's own icon from the browser tab; on a personal Gmail account, Google's icon, as the Suite manual says.

On Google Workspace, your Workspace admin can also put the Hub in the Google apps menu, the grid at the top right of Gmail and Drive, the same way as the apps (Suite manual: In the Google apps menu). For the Hub, the name under the tile is Six7 Hub and the Entity ID is six7-hub.

  • "Sign in with your work account" means the Hub couldn't tell who you are. Use a browser profile where you're signed in only with your work account.
  • "You don’t have access to the Six7 Hub" names the Admins to ask.
  • "The Six7 Hub isn’t set up yet" means the Owner account hasn't run Six7 › Setup / repair in the Hub's Sheet yet.

2. Reading My apps#

Each card is one of your company's Six7 apps: the app's tile and name, the install's name and version, a light and a word, and what to do next.

The light comes from two things the Hub reads at each check: what the app says of itself in its Sheet (working, parked, retired or wiped), and what its web address shows when the Hub opens it as the Owner account. A light appears only on that evidence. An app the Hub couldn't check is grey, never green.

LightWordWhat it meansWhat to do
GreenWorkingAt the last check, the app's address showed the app itselfNothing
YellowParkedNothing runs and its address shows a parked page. Its Sheet and files are untouchedBring it back online (§2.1)
RedRetiredNothing runs. Its Sheet and files are kept for referenceTo use the app again, ask Six7 for a new install
✕WipedIts Sheet and files are in Google's trash, gone for good 30 days after the wipeNothing: the line stays for the record
Orange "!"Not workingIt should be working but isn't: it asks for Setup / repair, its address answers "not found", it has no web address yet, or its Sheet is in the trash while it still runsThe card says what to do and who does it. If it doesn't fit, ask Six7 for support
GreyNot checkedNo evidence yet: not checked yet, the check couldn't tell, or its Sheet isn't in the Owner account's Drive any moreRefresh list checks it again. A Sheet that went missing: §5.1

Under the word, the card says since when and by whom (Parked, Retired, Wiped), or when it was checked.

  • Open opens the app in a new tab; a wiped app has none.
  • Admins also see Sheet, which opens the app's Sheet, and Hide (§5.2).
  • Members see the apps that aren't hidden, that the last refresh found, and that aren't retired or wiped. On anything but a green card, a Member's card says to ask an Admin. Admins see every app; a hidden or missing one shows dimmed.

2.1 Bring a parked app back online#

  1. Sign in as the Owner account and open the app's Sheet: Sheet on its card.
  2. Click Six7 › Danger zone › Resume.
  3. Type the Sheet's name and click OK.

The app's address shows the app again at once. The next check here turns the card green.

2.2 When the Hub checks#

Every Refresh list checks each app, and so does the nightly refresh. A park or a resume done in an app's own Sheet shows at the next check.

The Hub checks only your company's own apps. It opens their addresses as the Owner account, inside your domain, or inside your own account on a personal Gmail, and it contacts nothing outside it. Google's permission screen calls this "Connect to an external service", because to Google every web address is external. The Hub uses it only for your own apps.

Under the apps, the card Something wrong, or a question? leads to Ask Six7 for support.

3. Asking Six7 for support#

  1. Click Ask Six7 for support.
  2. In App, choose the app it's about, or General (not about one app). The Hub adds the app's version and address, so Six7 knows which install you mean.
  3. Write a Subject, up to 120 characters.
  4. In Details, say what happened, what you expected, and when. Steps to see it help most. Up to 5,000 characters.
  5. Choose the Urgency:
    • Low: A question or an idea.
    • Normal: Something isn’t working as it should.
    • High: People can’t work.
  6. Click Send.

The Hub shows "Sent to Six7 support" with the request's number, for example REQ-0007. See your requests opens Requests.

Note

For a setup problem, put the summary of Six7 › Setup / repair in the details. It shows which line is marked ✗.

3.1 What Six7 receives#

One email, sent from your Hub's Owner account under your company's name. Its subject starts with "[Six7 support]", then your domain, the app and your subject. It carries your details, the app's version and address, and the Hub's version.

Its Reply-To is your address, so Six7's answer comes straight to you.

3.2 Who can ask, and how often#

  • Admins can always ask.
  • Members can ask when an Admin sets support.allUsers to TRUE. Otherwise the form says "Only Admins can ask Six7 for support from this Hub." and names the Admins.
  • The Hub sends up to 20 requests a day (support.dailyLimit). After that, it asks you to try again tomorrow.
  • Requests go to Six7's support address, support.address, which Six7 sets when it installs the Hub. If it's ever blank, the form says so and the next Setup / repair fills it in.

3.3 When sending fails#

The Hub says the email couldn't be sent, gives the request's number, and keeps it in Requests as Failed. Try again later. If it keeps failing, an Admin runs Check installation (§5.3).

4. Requests#

Requests lists what was sent from this Hub, newest first. Admins see every request, with who sent it. Members see their own.

  • Result is Sent when the email left, or Failed when Google refused it.
  • Click a subject to read the request in full: when it was sent, by whom, to which address, and the details.
  • Newer and Older move between pages of 100.

5. For Admins#

5.1 Refresh list#

The Hub finds your apps in the Owner account's Drive. A Sheet counts as an app when:

  • the Owner account owns it;
  • its name contains "Six7";
  • it isn't in the trash;
  • Setup / repair has run in it.

The Hub itself, and any other Hub, are never listed.

The Hub refreshes the list every night between 00:00 and 01:00. Refresh list on My apps refreshes it now. The line above the cards says when it last ran, who ran it and what it found, for example "4 installs found."

A refresh never removes an app. One whose Sheet went to the Owner account's trash is still read there, with its state. One it can't find at all shows grey, Not checked, "not found since" a date, until it's found again. Check whether its Sheet:

  1. is in Drive's trash: restore it;
  2. was renamed without "Six7": put "Six7" back in its name;
  3. now belongs to another account: ask Six7.

Then click Refresh list.

5.2 Hide and List it again#

Hide takes an app off the Members' list, for example a test copy. Admins still see it, marked "Hidden: Members don’t see it". List it again shows it to Members again.

5.3 Check installation#

Admin tools › Check installation checks the Hub. It runs each time you open Admin tools. It looks at:

  • its files and versions, permissions and email;
  • its time zones, nightly job and owner;
  • its web address, browser tab icon, tabs and columns;
  • that it checks your apps only inside your domain or account;
  • the support address, who can ask, the customer's name and the list of apps;
  • when the Hub is parked, since when and by whom.

Each line starts with ✓ (fine), ⚠ (worth a look) or ✗ (to fix). Fix the lines marked ✗, or send Six7 a screenshot.

5.4 Settings#

The settings are in the Hub Sheet's _Settings tab. Change only the Value column.

SettingDefaultWhat it does
support.addressSix7's support addressWhere Ask Six7 for support sends requests. Six7 sets it at install; Setup / repair fills it when it's blank
support.allUsersFALSETRUE: everyone who can open the Hub can ask Six7 for support. FALSE: only Admins
support.dailyLimit20The most support requests the Hub sends a day. 0 turns requests off
customer.nameblankYour company's name, in the sender name of support emails. Your domain when blank
access.unlistedMemberThe role of people in your domain who aren't in _Users: Member or None

5.5 People#

The _Users tab lists people by Email, Name, Role and Active. Role is Admin or Member. Untick Active to take someone's access away. The Owner account is always an Admin, so it can't lock itself out.

Team

People in your domain who aren't listed open the Hub as Members, unless access.unlisted is None.

5.6 The Six7 menu#

The Hub's Sheet has a Six7 menu for the Owner account:

  • Open app opens the web app.
  • Setup / repair sets up or repairs the Hub. Its summary ends with "Setup complete (version …)."
  • Refresh list refreshes My apps.
  • Check installation runs the checks in §5.3.
  • Danger zone › Park and Resume stop and restart the Hub itself (§5.7). Retire and Wipe come with a later version: until then they say so and point at Park.

5.7 Park the Hub itself#

Park stops the Hub until you resume it. Your apps keep running: parking the Hub changes nothing in them.

  1. Sign in as the Owner account and open the Hub's Sheet.
  2. Click Six7 › Danger zone › Park. A box says what will happen.
  3. Type the Sheet's name and click OK.

While the Hub is parked:

  • its address shows one line to everyone: "The Six7 Hub is parked. Ask the owner, …, to resume it.";
  • the nightly refresh doesn't run, and Setup / repair installs no job;
  • its Sheet, its list of apps and its history stay as they are.

The Owner account gets an email with the summary, which ends with Check installation's lines.

To bring it back, do the same with Six7 › Danger zone › Resume. The Hub's address opens the Hub again at once, and its nightly job is installed again.

6. Files your apps add to your Drive#

Your Six7 apps add a few files to the Owner account's Drive on their own. Nobody at Six7 makes them by hand, and Six7 doesn't look at them. This chapter says what they are, when they appear and why.

6.1 The audit files: what, when and why#

What. Every Six7 app keeps a history of changes in its Sheet's _Audit tab: who changed what, when, and the values before and after. Once a month, each app moves the older part of that history into a separate spreadsheet: the audit file for that year.

Why. A Sheet slows down as a tab grows. Moving old rows out keeps your app fast. Nothing is deleted: the rows only move.

When. Each app does it on its own, at night between 00:00 and 01:00, on the 1st to the 7th of each month. It moves the rows dated before the first day of the previous month. A new file appears the first time an app moves rows of a given year:

CaseWhen the audit file first appears
A new app, in use from OctoberEarly December, with October's history
An app loaded with demo dataThe first night between the 1st and the 7th of a month, after loading. Demo history is back-dated, so a file for the year before can appear the same night
Every app, each new yearEarly March, with January's history, in that year's new file

That's why an audit file can seem to appear from nowhere: it shows up on the first nights of a month, while nobody is working.

Who can see them. Only the Owner account: the files are never shared. Six7 has no standing access to your account (Suite manual §7).

The Hub also keeps one small file there, Six7 Hub icon.png: the house in your browser tab. Setup / repair makes it once and shares it with your company to view, so browsers can show it. It is the only file the Hub can change; the Hub only reads the rest of your Drive. If it's deleted, tabs show Google's icon until the next Setup / repair makes it again. On a personal Google account the Hub makes no icon, and tabs show Google's.

Each file has one tab, Audit, with the same columns as _Audit. The year in its name is the year of the changes inside.

6.3 Other files that come and go#

  • Service Desk messages. In the first week of each month, the Service Desk moves the messages of tickets closed more than 90 days ago. They go to the audit file of the year the ticket closed, on a second tab, Messages. The ticket stays, with a note that starts "Older messages archived" and names the file.
  • Your apps' browser tab icons. Each app keeps one small image in its Files folder, such as Six7 Kanban icon.png: the icon in your browser tab. Setup / repair makes it once and shares it with your company to view, so browsers can show it, like the Hub's own (§6.2). If you delete it, the tab shows Google's icon until the next Setup / repair makes it again. On a personal Gmail account there's no company to share it with: the tabs show Google's icon, and you may find the file in Drive's trash.
  • Report files. When you make a report, the app saves its files in the Reports folder inside its Files folder. Each night between 02:00 and 03:00, it moves report files older than 7 days to the trash. Save a copy elsewhere to keep one.

Important

Keep your own files out of the Reports folders. In Kanban and the Service Desk, the nightly clean-up moves every file there older than 7 days to the trash.

6.4 Can we delete them?#

You can, but we recommend keeping them. No app reads its audit files, so deleting one breaks nothing. The history it held is gone for good, though.

If you delete one, empty it from Drive's trash too, with Delete forever. Each app finds its audit file by its name in its Files folder, and the Hub remembers its own. A file left in the trash may keep receiving rows on the first nights of a month, then disappear when the trash is emptied.
