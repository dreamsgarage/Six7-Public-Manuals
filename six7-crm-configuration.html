1. Before you start#

1.1 What the CRM does for you#

Six7 CRM keeps your companies, contacts and deals in one place, with no duplicates. It shows the pipeline as a board where a deal moves with one drag, records calls, emails, meetings, notes and tasks, and tells each salesperson what to do today. Reports show the pipeline per quarter, how it evolved, and what was won and lost.

People open it from one link. The main screens are My day, Pipeline, Deals, Contacts, Companies and Reports, plus Admin tools for managers and Admins. The CRM User manual shows each screen.

1.2 What Six7 has installed#

Everything is in the owner account (§1.3):

PartWhat it is
The SheetA Google Sheet named like Six7 CRM — your company. It holds the companies, contacts, deals, activities, settings and users
The scriptThe CRM's seven files, attached to the Sheet (Extensions › Apps Script)
The web appThe link people open. The Sheet's Six7 › Open app opens it too
The Files folderSix7 CRM — Files in the owner's Drive, with a Reports folder inside for report files
TriggersFour timed jobs: the nightly job (00:00–01:00) that takes pipeline snapshots, a cleanup of report files (02:00–03:00), a daily job (05:00–06:00), and the digest at 07:00 by default. Two more notice changes typed into the Sheet. Times are in the Sheet's time zone

The general model is in Suite §3.

1.3 Your owner account Solo#

The CRM is installed in your own @gmail.com account. On a personal account the CRM is for you alone, as its Admin: Google can't tell the app who other people are outside a Workspace domain, so only you can open it. Moving to Google Workspace later makes it a team app (Suite §4).

Expect a few things that differ from a Workspace install:

  • Google shows "This app isn't verified" the first time you run Setup / repair (§2.1).
  • The setup summary shows ✗ for the browser tab icon. That's expected: a personal account can't share the icon with a company, so the tab shows Google's icon.
  • A personal account can send fewer emails a day (§6.3).

1.3 Your owner account Team#

The CRM runs as a dedicated owner account in your Workspace, usually a shared one such as six7-admin@yourcompany.com (Suite §5). It needs a Google Workspace licence and 2-Step Verification, is used only for Six7, and nobody works from it day to day.

Apps Script must be on for the owner account's part of your organisation. It's on by default.

1.4 What the Workspace admin may need to do Team#

Your Google Workspace admin may be asked to:

  • let Six7 deliver the app: in the Admin console, API controls may need to trust clasp, the Google tool Six7 uses during a delivery window (Suite §8);
  • make the app easy to find, once it's live: a short address or an intranet link, a tile in the Google apps menu, or a managed browser bookmark. Six7 provides the app's icons.

2. First sign-in and setup#

2.1 Run Setup / repair#

Six7 installs the app. Then the owner account finishes it:

  1. Open the Sheet as the owner account.
  2. Click Six7 › Setup / repair.
  3. Google asks the owner account to allow the app. Click Advanced, then **Go to Six7 CRM — your company** (it says unsafe, because the app is your own private copy and isn't published by Google), tick Select all, then click Continue. A run with a permission left unticked stops and asks again.
  4. Reload the Sheet when it's done.

Setup / repair creates the tabs, the Files folder and the triggers, and adds the owner account as the first Admin. Only the owner account can run it. It's safe to run again: it only adds what's missing, and a second run starts with ✓ Already up to date.

2.2 Read the setup summary#

Setup / repair ends with a summary:

  • one line per change, marked ✓, ⚠ (worth a look) or ✗ (a step that couldn't run);
  • then the Check installation lines (§2.3);
  • and "Setup complete (version 0.1.0)." last.

If a line shows ✗, run Setup / repair once more. If it still shows ✗, send Six7 a screenshot (Suite §10).

Solo The ✗ for the tab icon is expected on a personal account (§1.3).

2.3 Check installation#

Six7 › Check installation in the Sheet, or Admin tools › Check installation in the app, checks the install at any time: the files and their versions, the services the app needs, the time zones, the triggers (all 6), the folders and the tab icon, the owner and the app's address. In the Sheet each line is ✓, ⚠ (worth a look) or ✗; the app shows only ✓ and ✗.

Run it after every update and whenever something seems wrong.

2.4 Open the app for the first time#

  1. As the owner account, click Six7 › Open app in the Sheet. Before the first visit, it says how to open the web app once from the script editor.
  2. The first visit records the app's address (app.url). Emails and copied links use it from then on.
  3. Bookmark the link. Sign in as described in Suite §5.

As an Admin you see Admin tools after Reports. It has two tabs: Tools and Users.

3. Company settings#

Settings live in the Sheet's _Settings tab: one row per setting, with Key, Value and Description. Change only the Value cell. Admin tools links straight to _Settings, _Users and _Lists. After changing digest.hour, run Six7 › Setup / repair so the digest moves to the new hour.

4. Users and roles#

4.1 You are the Admin Solo#

On a personal account you are the CRM's only user and its Admin. You own every company, contact and deal, and you see every screen. There's nobody to add, no regions and no reassigning. Skip to §5.

When your company takes on people, the CRM moves to Google Workspace and this chapter applies (Suite §4).

4.2 Roles in the CRM Team#

RoleTypical personSeesEditsExtra
Sales RepSalesperson, account managerThe records they own, plus everything under the companies they ownTheir own scopeNew records are theirs
Operations ManagerOperations lead for a city or regionEverything under companies in their region, and the records of people in their region. With no region: everythingTheir scopeReassign owners, Validate data and Import pasted rows, for their region; the report email for their region

Only Operations Managers, Sales Managers and Admins can change a record's owner to someone else. There can be several of each role.

4.3 Add, change and remove people Team#

In the app: Admin tools › Users › Add user, or Edit on a person's row. Or one row per person in the _Users tab:

ColumnWhat to set
EmailTheir work address, in your company's domain
NameTheir name as people know it
RoleSales Rep, Operations Manager, Sales Manager or Admin
RegionOne of the Region list. Blank for the Sales Manager, and for an Operations Manager who covers the whole country
ActiveTicked

ReportEmailOff, Daily or Weekly. Only Sales Managers, Operations Managers and Admins receive it. Add user in Admin tools makes it Weekly for Sales and Operations Managers unless you choose otherwise; a blank cell typed into _Users means Off

The owner account always stays an active Admin.

When someone leaves:

  1. In Admin tools › Tools, use Reassign owner: choose who they are (From, which includes inactive people) and who takes over (To), tick what moves (companies, contacts, open deals, open tasks), then Show what moves and confirm. Only what the person owns moves. Records other people own under a moved company keep their owner, and the new company owner sees them. Won and lost deals and done tasks keep their original owner, for reports.
  2. Untick their Active box. Don't delete the row. An inactive person can't open the CRM.

4.4 People you didn't add Team#

The access.unlisted setting decides what happens when someone in your company opens the CRM without a row in _Users:

  • None (the default): they see "You don't have access to Six7 CRM yet", and a list of the Admins to ask.
  • Sales Rep: they're let in as a Sales Rep. They own nothing yet, so they see only what they create.

People outside your company's domain never get in.

4.5 Scope and visibility Team#

Who sees a record follows its Owner, and for companies, the company's Region:

  • A Sales Rep sees a company if they own it, and then everything under it: its contacts, deals and activities.
  • A Sales Rep who owns a deal or contact under someone else's company sees only that company's name, domain, industry and country.
  • An Operations Manager sees the companies in their region and everything under them, plus whatever people in their region own. A company with no region is seen only through its owner. Validate data lists companies without a region.
  • The Sales Manager and Admins see everything.

Every screen, search, report, link and email follows these rules.

Example: Diego, a Sales Rep in Buenos Aires, owns Globex Retail. He sees its contacts and deals, and so does Nicolás, the Buenos Aires Operations Manager. Valeria, a Sales Rep in Córdoba, doesn't. If she tries to add Globex Retail again, the app tells her it exists and who owns it, and nothing more.

4.6 Backup Admin and Sheet editors Team#

  • Give a second person the Admin role and edit access to the Sheet, so someone can stand in if the owner account is unavailable.
  • Don't share the Sheet, the Files folder or the Reports folder with anyone else. People use the app, and the app shares each report file with the person who asked for it. See Suite §7.

5. Your data#

5.1 Start empty, with demo data, or with your own#

  • Empty: people add companies, contacts and deals in the app.
  • Your own data, from spreadsheets or Google Contacts: §5.2.
  • Demo data: a year of a fictional company's sales, for trying the CRM out. Six7 loads it only into a demo install, never into the one your company will use, because the app has no step to remove it.

5.2 Import from a spreadsheet#

  1. In the Sheet, check File › Settings › Locale, so the dates and numbers you paste are read the right way. The import refuses dates and numbers it can't read.
  2. Paste rows into the tabs below their header, in this order: Companies, Contacts, Deals, Activities. Leave ID and the system columns blank. Columns are matched by their header.
  3. Set Owner on every row to the salesperson's email. A row without one is owned by whoever runs the import.
  4. For contacts, you can type the company's name in CompanyName instead of its ID. The import links the contact to that company, or creates the company if there's none.
  5. In Admin tools (or the Six7 menu), run Validate data. It lists problems by tab and row and changes nothing. Fix them.
  6. Run Import pasted rows. Rows that pass are imported; the skipped ones are listed.
  7. After importing deals, run Setup / repair once more. If no pipeline snapshot was taken at go-live, it takes the first one now, so reports start from your real pipeline.

From Google Contacts: at contacts.google.com, Export › Google CSV, then copy these columns into Contacts:

5.3 Export and backups#

  • Google keeps the Sheet's history. File › Version history shows earlier versions and can restore one.
  • A copy for safekeeping: File › Make a copy saves a snapshot of the data. Never ask people to use a copy: it has no app and no triggers.
  • Exports from the app: every list and report has Export to Sheets, and reports also Generate report (Doc) and Download PDF. A Sheet or Doc opens on Google's Make a copy page, which saves it in the person's own Drive; the temporary file in the Reports folder is deleted after reports.retentionDays.
  • Nothing is deleted. Archived records stay in the Sheet. Each month, audit rows older than the month before move to a spreadsheet of their own, Six7 CRM — Audit and the year.
  • Pipeline history starts on go-live day: reports can't show the pipeline before the first snapshot.

6.1 What the app sends and when#

EmailWho gets itWhen
Daily digest "your day"Everyone with Digest tickedAt digest.hour, only when there's something: the person's own tasks overdue or due today, meetings today, follow-ups due, stale deals and close dates passed. Managers get theirs too; the whole picture is in the pipeline overview

6.2 Turning them on or off#

  • The digest: an Admin ticks or unticks Digest for each person, in Admin tools › Users or _Users.
  • The report email: an Admin sets ReportEmail.
  • Admin tools › Send report email now sends the pipeline overview at once, to every recipient or to you only, to see what it looks like. Run reminders now runs the whole routine: snapshots due, digests, report emails and cleanup.

Solo You get your own digest and, if you set ReportEmail, your own pipeline overview. Nobody else is emailed.

6.3 Daily quotas#

Every email counts against the owner account's daily limit, set by Google. Check installation shows what's left today.

Team

A Workspace account can send to about 1,500 recipients a day: a digest per person and the report emails fit easily.

Solo

A personal account can send to about 100 recipients a day. The CRM sends you a few.

Emails over the limit are skipped, not sent later. Records and tasks stay in the app, and the next day's emails go out as usual.

9.2 Updates from Six7#

Six7 delivers updates through a delivery window, as in Suite §8. After an update:

  1. Run Six7 › Setup / repair as the owner account. It adds what the new version needs. If it asks for a new permission, allow it.
  2. Check that the app's footer shows the new version.
  3. Run Check installation.

9.3 Going back to the previous version#

Ask Six7. It's done like an update: the app's address stays the same, and your data stays as it is, because Setup / repair only ever adds to the Sheet.

9.4 Security and privacy#

On top of Suite §7:

  • Salespeople's privacy: a Sales Rep can't reach records outside their scope through any screen, search, link, email or report. A record they can't see is reported as not found, so the app doesn't even confirm it exists.
  • Duplicates owned by others: the warning names the owner, and nothing else.
  • Who changed what: the _Audit tab records every change, and each deal shows its History.
  • Email and Calendar links open the person's own Gmail and Calendar. The CRM asks for no Gmail or Calendar permission.

9.5 Stop, park or remove the app#

The CRM stops from the Danger zone, at the bottom of the Sheet's Six7 menu: Park, Resume, Retire (keep the Sheet) and Wipe. Only the owner account can use them; anyone else gets "Only the owner account can run Park."

All four are in this version: Park and Resume stop the CRM for a while and bring it back; Retire closes it for good and keeps everything it holds in one folder; Wipe removes it and everything it made, to Google's trash.

Park stops the CRM until you resume it, and keeps everything:

  • it deletes its six triggers: Nightly, Cleanup, Daily job, Digest and report emails, On edit and On change;
  • its address answers one line to everyone: "Six7 CRM is parked. Ask the owner, the owner account's address, to resume it.";
  • no email, digest or report goes out, and nothing runs in the background;
  • the Sheet, the Files folder and every report stay as they are.
  1. As the owner account, open the Sheet and click Six7 › Danger zone › Park.
  2. Read what will happen, type the Sheet's name to confirm, and click OK. Capitals, spaces and the kind of dash don't count: a plain - works for the — in Six7 CRM — your company. With any other name, nothing changes: "That isn't the Sheet's name, so nothing was changed."
  3. The summary ends with "Parked (version 0.1.0)." The owner account also gets it by email, "Six7 CRM was parked", and _Audit records a PARK row. If a step failed, the last line adds "Fix the lines marked ✗, then run Check installation again."

While it's parked:

  • Everyone who opens the address sees only the parked line, Admins too, so Admin tools can't be reached. A page that was already open shows the same line at its next click.
  • Setup / repair still repairs the install, so Six7 can deliver an update to a parked CRM, but it installs no trigger: "⚠ Parked since … by …: no trigger was installed. Six7 › Danger zone › Resume brings it back."
  • Check installation starts with "⚠ State: Parked since …" and shows "✓ Triggers: none, as a parked install should have". If a trigger was left behind, it names it, and Park again removes it.
  • Validate data starts with "Parked since … by …".
  • In the Sheet's Six7 menu, Run reminders now and Send report email now don't run: "Run reminders now doesn't run: Six7 CRM is parked. Six7 › Danger zone › Resume brings it back." The rest of the menu still works for the owner account: Setup / repair, Check installation, Validate data, Import pasted rows and Open app.

Resume brings the CRM back, at the same address:

  1. As the owner account: Six7 › Danger zone › Resume, and type the Sheet's name.
  2. It installs the six triggers again, with the digest at digest.hour, and refreshes the app's copies of the data tabs, so anything typed into the Sheet while parked shows at once. The address opens the app again for everyone with access, and emails, the digest and reports go out again from their next run.
  3. The summary ends with "Resumed (version 0.1.0)." The owner account gets "Six7 CRM was resumed", and _Audit records a RESUME row.

Retire (keep the Sheet) closes the CRM for good and keeps everything it holds in one folder. Use it when your company stops using the CRM. You can retire a live or a parked CRM. Retire is final: to use the CRM again, ask Six7 for a new install.

  1. As the owner account: Six7 › Danger zone › Retire (keep the Sheet). Read what will happen, type the Sheet's name to confirm, and click OK.
  2. Retire works in seven steps:
    • it writes an audit row RETIRE, then stops the app as Park does and deletes its triggers;
    • it makes the folder Six7 CRM — Retired followed by the date, next to the Files folder, and moves the Files folder into it, with the Audit files and Reports;
    • it saves one PDF per report, R-CRM-1 to R-CRM-5 and S-06, for the whole company as of that day;
    • it writes the Retirement statement, as a PDF and as retirement-statement.json: each tab's rows with a SHA-256 fingerprint anyone can recompute, the audit trail's numbers, and every file in the folder;
    • it moves the Sheet into the folder and records the CRM as retired.
  3. The summary ends with "Retired (version 0.1.0) — statement:" and the statement's link. The owner account also gets it by email, "Six7 CRM was retired". Nothing is deleted.

Two steps stay with you afterwards, and the summary lists them under "Left to do by hand":

  • close the address: open the Sheet › Extensions › Apps Script › Deploy › Manage deployments › the web app › Archive;
  • remove the app's access: https://myaccount.google.com/connections › the app › Remove.

If the Files folder or its Reports folder is missing, Retire doesn't start, and says so: run Six7 › Setup / repair, then Retire. While a Retire is under way, Setup / repair waits: "Retire is under way: setup waits until it ends. Six7 › Danger zone › Retire carries it on." A big CRM can take more than one run: the summary then says "Retiring: step … of 7 is next. It carries on by itself in a minute, and the owner's email brings the final summary." Until then the address answers the parked page, and Park and Resume refuse. If a step fails, Retire stops there and says what to fix; run Retire again and it carries on from that step, without making anything twice.

After Retire, the address answers one line to everyone: "Six7 CRM was retired on the date." The Sheet's Six7 menu keeps one item, About this retired install: when and by whom it was retired, and a link to the Retirement statement. Anyone who can open the Sheet can use it, auditors included; it only shows. The retired CRM stays exactly as Retire left it, so its statement stays true: Setup / repair doesn't run on it ("Six7 CRM was retired on …: setup doesn't change a retired install, so its Retirement statement stays true."), and it's never wiped.

Wipe removes the CRM and everything it made. The app never deletes anything forever: each item goes to Google's trash, which empties itself after 30 days. You can wipe a live or a parked CRM, never a retired one: "Wipe doesn't run on a retired install: Retire is final, and its folder is the record it keeps."

Before you go on, close the address: open the Sheet › Extensions › Apps Script › Deploy › Manage deployments › the web app › Archive. Once the Sheet is in the trash, Apps Script asks to take the project out of the trash before it lets you.

  1. As the owner account: Six7 › Danger zone › Wipe. The first screen lists the triggers and every file and folder that goes to the trash, with links, the Sheet last. Type the Sheet's name and click OK.
  2. Then type WIPE, exactly, in capitals: "Last check. Type WIPE to move Six7 CRM and everything listed to Google's trash now. Nothing can be brought back from the app; Drive keeps it in the trash for 30 days." Anything else changes nothing: "That isn't the word WIPE, so nothing was changed."
  3. Wipe works in this order:
    • it writes an audit row WIPE and emails the list of what goes to the trash, with its links, to the owner account ("Six7 CRM is being wiped"): after Wipe, that email is the only copy left;
    • it deletes every trigger;
    • it moves the Reports folder, the Audit files and the Files folder to the trash;
    • it records the CRM as wiped, so the address answers "Six7 CRM was removed on the date.";
    • last, it moves the Sheet to the trash.
  4. The summary ends with "Wiped (version 0.1.0)." and lists what's left to do by hand: close the address, if you haven't; if 30 days in the trash is too long, Drive › Trash › the Sheet and the folders › Delete forever, with no way back from that; and remove the app's access at https://myaccount.google.com/connections › the app › Remove.

If the email to the owner can't go, Wipe stops before changing anything else. If a trigger can't be deleted or a file can't be moved, Wipe stops before it records the state or moves the Sheet, and parks the CRM, so nothing runs: "Wipe stopped before it recorded the state Wiped or moved this Sheet: fix what the ✗ lines say, then run Six7 › Danger zone › Wipe again. It skips what is in the trash already." If only the Sheet can't be moved, move it by hand: File › Move to trash. When a step fails, the owner account also gets the summary by email.

A wiped Sheet, opened from the trash, has no Six7 menu, and Setup / repair doesn't run on it.

Closing the address is optional, and only by hand. Park keeps the address, so anyone who opens it reads the parked line. To close it: open the Sheet › Extensions › Apps Script › Deploy › Manage deployments, choose the web app, and Archive it. Opening the CRM again then needs a new address: after Resume, make a new deployment, clear app.url in _Settings, and open the new address once as the owner account. Six7 can do it with you in a delivery window. Old bookmarks and links in past emails keep pointing at the closed address.

I trashed the Sheet. Has the CRM stopped? No. Its address still opens the app, and its triggers are still installed. Restore the Sheet from Drive's trash, Park it and close its address, then trash it again if you want to. Or, without restoring it, delete its triggers as the owner account at https://script.google.com/home/triggers: ⋮ › Delete trigger on each one. That stops its jobs, but its address keeps opening the app until you restore the Sheet and close the address. The triggers page names each row by its project only, and two installs can share a name, so check ⋮ › Project details before you delete.

"Six7 CRM was removed on …"The owner account wiped the CRM: everything it made went to Google's trash, which keeps it for 30 days (§9.5). If that was a mistake, ask Six7 at once

11. Getting help#

Contact Six7 as in Suite §10. For the CRM, also send:

  • the record's ID if it's about one record, for example DEAL-0016 (on the deal page, above its name);
  • the person's role and region, if it's about what someone can or can't see;
  • the Check installation lines, if it's about the install.
