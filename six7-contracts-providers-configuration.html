1. Before you start#

1.1 What Contracts & Providers does for you#

Six7 Contracts & Providers keeps your company's contracts, providers and purchase requests in one place:

  • Contracts with their dates, value and exit terms. The app warns before renewal and notice dates pass, and decides by your own rules which renewals simply roll over and which need an approval, before the notice deadline.
  • Providers in one directory, with their compliance documents (insurance, tax certificates, NDAs) and when they expire.
  • Obligations: what a contract commits you to, such as a payment or a review, with an owner and a due date.
  • Purchase requests, for a one-off purchase or for fees over months or years, routed by your rules to the right approvers by their total value, their term and what ending them early would cost.
  • Reports on renewals, committed spend, approvals, compliance and spend by provider, and an audit trail of every change.

People open it from one link. The screens are Home, Approvals, Contracts, Providers, Obligations, Purchases and Reports, plus Admin tools for Procurement Managers and Admins. The Contracts & Providers User manual shows each screen.

Contracts & Providers runs as an account of its own in your Workspace, usually a shared one such as six7-admin@yourcompany.com (Suite §5). It needs a Google Workspace licence and 2-Step Verification, is used only for Six7, and nobody works from it day to day. It sends the app's emails and owns the Files folder.

Apps Script must be on for the owner account's part of your organisation. It's on by default. The app doesn't run on a personal @gmail.com account.

Your Google Workspace admin may be asked to:

  • allow sharing with everyone in the company: the browser tab shows the app's icon from a file shared with the company. Where that isn't allowed, the tab shows Google's icon instead;
  • make the app easy to find, once it's live: a short address such as contracts.yourcompany.com, a tile in the Google apps menu, or a managed browser bookmark. Six7 provides the app's icons.

1.5 Will it fit how you work?#

Contracts & Providers works in a set way, shown step by step in the User manual's chapter 4. Before you set it up, compare that with how your company handles contracts and purchases today.

What you choose:

You chooseWhere
Who approves: the people of Group 1 (standard) and Group 2 (senior)§3.1, §3.4
Which purchases are approved automatically, and which go to Group 1 or Group 2, by type, total contract value, term and what ending them early costs§3.4, §3.5
Which renewals roll over on their own and which need an approval, and how many months before the end each review opens§3.4, §3.5
How far ahead renewals, notice deadlines, expiring documents and obligations show§3.2
Your currency, financial year, contract types, provider categories and document types§3.1, §3.3
Who sees everything (Procurement Managers) and who sees only their own records (Individuals)§4.2
Who gets the daily digest, and the procurement overview, daily or weekly§6

What this release fixes:

  • One approval step, by two groups. Any one approver of the group decides. There are no approval chains and no several approvers on one item.
  • Nobody approves their own purchase request, or the renewal of a contract they own.
  • Purchase requests go Draft, Submitted, Approved or Rejected, Ordered, Received, Closed; or Cancelled. Contracts are Draft, Under review, Active, Expired, Terminated or Renewed.
  • The daily job opens renewal reviews and rolls contracts over or ends them. Nobody opens a review by hand.
  • Amounts aren't converted between currencies. The rules and the charts use your default currency, and an automatic approval applies only to requests in it.
  • Provider documents are seen only by Procurement Managers and Admins.
  • People of your company's domain only. Providers and guests don't use the app.
  • No budgets, purchase orders, invoices or e-signature: a contract keeps a link to its signed copy.
  • English only.

Three questions to ask of your current process:

  1. Who approves what today? Write it down as amounts, terms and "can we end it without cost". That becomes your rules (§3.4).
  2. How early do you need to know about a renewal? A big contract can take 9 to 12 months to renegotiate or replace; a small one, 3.
  3. Who keeps your providers' certificates, and who may see them? Here only Procurement Managers and Admins see provider documents; everyone else sees whether a provider is compliant.

3.4 Plan your approvals#

Plan the rules on paper before you change them. Start from your company's approval policy, even if today it lives only in people's heads.

Answer these first:

  1. Who approves the everyday purchases, and who the big ones? They become Group 1 and Group 2. A group can have several people, and any one of them decides.
  2. Up to what amount is a one-off purchase simply allowed? That's the automatic approval: by default, under 50.
  3. Where are the limits between the groups, for a one-off purchase, for fees over a year, and for commitments of several years?
  4. Does it matter whether a commitment can be ended without cost? A 3-year internet line you can cancel at any time is a smaller risk than a 3-year lease you must pay to the end.
  5. Which renewals may roll over without anyone deciding, and which need someone senior? And how many months ahead must that decision start?

The model. Each purchase request, when it's submitted, and each contract's renewal, when its review opens, is checked against the rules of its kind, from the top. The first rule whose conditions all hold decides where it goes: approved or renewed automatically, Group 1 or Group 2. A blank condition means "any". If no rule matches, Group 2 decides.

What a rule can look at:

ConditionIn a purchase ruleIn a renewal rule
The typePurchaseType: Single purchase or RecurringContractType: Vendor, Lease and so on
MaxTCV: the value is at most thisThe request's total contract value: the price, or the setup fee plus the fee × the payments in the termThe value of one renewal term: the fee × the payments in the renewal term
MaxTermMonths: the term is at most this many monthsThe committed term. A single purchase has no term and always passesThe renewal term
RequiresNoLiability: it can be ended without costIf we end it early is No cost to end it. A single purchase always passesThe contract's exit terms are None

The outcomes:

OutcomeForWhat happens
Auto-approvePurchasesThe request is approved at once, and only the requester is emailed. It applies only to requests in defaults.currency; others go on down the list
Auto-renewRenewalsNo approval. The review is recorded as Auto-approved: an auto-renewing contract rolls over at its end date, and the owner renews any other with Renew
Group 1BothThe standard approvers decide
Group 2BothThe senior approvers decide

The review's lead time. Each renewal rule says how many months before a contract's end date its review opens (ReviewLeadMonths): by default 3 for small contracts, and 9 or 12 for big ones. A review always opens at least 30 days before the notice deadline. Example: a contract ending on 30 June 2027 with a 120-day notice period must be given notice by 2 March. A 3-month lead would open its review on 30 March, too late; so it opens on 31 January, 30 days before the deadline.

Who decides. The approvers are the group's members, without the person who asks: the requester of a purchase, or the owner of the contract. If that leaves nobody:

  • a purchase request can't be submitted: "No approver is available for this request. Ask an admin to update the approval groups.";
  • a renewal goes to Group 2 instead. If Group 2 has nobody either, the review opens with no approver and the Admins are emailed. Nobody can decide that review, even after the groups change, so keep at least two people in each group.

Common policies:

Your policyIn the rules
"Small purchases need no approval"Keep P0 and set its MaxTCV to your limit. Rules say "at most", so "under 100" is 99.99
"Commitments of more than a year always go to a director"Give every Group 1 purchase rule a MaxTermMonths of 12 or less. Longer terms fall through to the catch-all, P9: Group 2
"Leases always need the senior approvers"A renewal rule with ContractType Lease and outcome Group 2, with the lowest Order
"Renewals we can cancel for free just roll over"Raise R1's MaxTCV
"One person approves everything"Keep only the catch-all rules, P9 and R9, and put them in Group 2 with a second person for their own requests

Traps to avoid:

  • Rules can only say "at most". To send big items somewhere, let the small ones match an earlier rule and the rest fall through to a later one.
  • The order is everything: a broad rule placed early catches what later rules were meant for. Try your rules with Test a scenario (§3.5) before saving.
  • Keep a catch-all rule, one with no conditions, last for each kind.
  • Approvers must be people in _Users, active: someone who can't open the app can't decide. Validate data doesn't check this.
  • Put at least two people in each group, so the approvers' own requests can be decided.

Before you type, have:

3.5 Approval and renewal rules#

Admins and Procurement Managers edit the rules in the app, on Admin tools › Rules: change a cell, Add rule or Remove, then Save rules. Or edit the _Rules tab, one row per rule.

ColumnWhat it holds
RuleIDA short label, such as P3, shown to approvers
OrderThe order the rules are checked in, lowest first, within each kind
ActiveUnticked rules are skipped
AppliesToPurchase or Renewal
PurchaseType, ContractType, MaxTCV, MaxTermMonths, RequiresNoLiabilityThe conditions (§3.4). Blank means any

ReviewLeadMonthsRenewal rules only: the months before the end date when the review opens. Blank uses renewals.defaultLeadMonths

  • Test a scenario, under the rules, says which rule would match a purchase or a renewal you describe, with the rules as they are on the screen, before you save them.
  • Add rule starts a rule at Order 100, after the catch-all rules P9 and R9 (Order 90): give it a lower Order, or it's never reached.
  • Save rules refuses a rule without a RuleID or an Order, or with an unknown AppliesTo or Outcome.
  • Rule warnings show above the rules and in Validate data: a missing or repeated RuleID, an Order that isn't a number, an unknown outcome, an outcome or condition of the wrong kind, and a missing catch-all rule. They never block the app.
  • Changes apply to new submissions and new reviews only. Decided items keep the rule they were decided under, and so do reviews already open.

When someone leaves:

  1. A Procurement Manager or Admin gives their contracts, providers and obligations to someone else: Edit on each one, and change its Owner. Access to the files moves with the owner.
  2. Take them out of approval.group1 and approval.group2.
  3. Untick their Active box. Don't delete the row. An inactive person can't open the app.

Requests and reviews already waiting for them keep them as an approver. If nobody else listed on one can decide it, a Procurement Manager cancels the request and the requester asks again; a renewal review stays pending until its end date, when it lapses.

4.4 People you didn't add#

The access.unlisted setting decides what happens when someone in your company opens the app without a row in _Users:

  • None (the default): they see "You don't have access to Six7 Contracts & Providers yet", and a list of the Admins to ask.
  • Individual: they're let in as an Individual. They see the provider directory and can ask for purchases. To own contracts, providers or obligations, they need a row in _Users.

People outside your company's domain never get in, and neither does anyone whose Active box is unticked.

4.5 Scope and visibility#

  • Individuals see the contracts they own and the obligations on them, obligations they own on other contracts, and the purchase requests they made.
  • Approvers also see the items routed to them, as in §4.2: a renewal's contract read-only.
  • Everyone sees the provider directory: name, category, status, contact, website, country, owner and compliance. A provider's tax ID and notes show only to its owner, Procurement Managers and Admins.
  • Provider documents are seen only by Procurement Managers and Admins, not even by the provider's owner.
  • Files follow their records. The app gives each file to exactly the people who may see its record, without "shared with you" emails: a contract's files to its owner and the approvers of its renewal reviews; a receipt to the requester and the approvers; and every file to Procurement Managers and Admins.
  • Procurement Managers and Admins see everything.

Every screen, search, export, report, link and email follows these rules.

Example: Tomás owns the Cloud ERP subscription. He sees it, its obligations and its renewal review. Laura, the Procurement Manager, sees it too, and decides its renewal because it was routed to Group 2. Sofía doesn't see it. But when Tomás asks for a fiber line that goes to Group 1, where Sofía is, she sees that request in full and decides it.

4.6 Backup Admin and Sheet editors#

Important

The script runs as the owner, so anyone who can edit the Sheet or its script can read every record, provider documents included. Only the owner account and one backup Admin may edit the Sheet; everyone else uses the app. Never share the Files folder or its subfolders either: the app gives each file to exactly the people allowed to see its record.

Give a second person the Admin role and edit access to the Sheet, so someone can stand in if the owner account is unavailable.

5. Your data#

5.1 Start empty, with demo data, or with your own#

  • Empty: people add contracts, providers and purchase requests in the app.
  • Your own data, from a contract spreadsheet or a vendor list: §5.2.
  • Demo data: a year of a fictional company's contracts, providers and purchases, for trying the app out. Six7 loads it only into a demo install, never into the one your company will use, because the app has no step to remove it.

5.2 Import from a spreadsheet#

Providers, provider documents, contracts and obligations can be imported. Purchase requests start in the app.

  1. In the Sheet, check File › Settings › Locale, so the dates and numbers you paste are read the right way. The import refuses dates and numbers it can't read.
  2. Add everyone who will own a record to _Users first (§4.3).
  3. Paste your providers into Providers, below the header. Leave ID and the system columns blank; columns are matched by their header. Set Owner to each one's owner.
  4. In Admin tools (or, as the owner account, the Six7 menu), run Validate data. It lists problems by tab and row and changes nothing. Fix them, then run Import pasted rows. Each provider gets its ID, such as PRV-0012.
  5. Paste the provider documents into ProviderDocs and the contracts into Contracts, with the new provider IDs in ProviderID. A contract with no provider, such as a customer's or a landlord's, needs its Counterparty instead. Set Status to Active for the contracts that are running: a contract without a status is imported as Draft, and gets no renewal review. Validate, then import.
  6. Paste the obligations into Obligations, with their contract's ID in ContractID. Validate, then import.

Use the exact values of _Lists and real dates. Every contract, provider and obligation needs an Owner who is in _Users. Rows that fail are skipped and listed; they stay in the Sheet for you to fix and import again. Rows with a blank ID are ignored by the app until they're imported.

Each imported row gets an ID, its computed columns (a contract's notice deadline and renewal value) and an audit row. The next daily job opens the renewal reviews that are already due. If it opens more than five for the same approver, they get one summary email instead of one each.

5.3 Export and backups#

  • Google keeps the Sheet's history. File › Version history shows earlier versions and can restore one.
  • A copy for safekeeping: File › Make a copy saves a snapshot of the data. Never ask people to use a copy: it has no app and no triggers.
  • Exports from the app: Contracts, Providers, Obligations, Purchases and Approvals have Export to Sheets, and reports also Generate report (Doc) and Download PDF. When a Sheet or Doc is ready, Open copy page opens Google's Make a copy page, which saves it in the person's own Drive; the temporary file in the Reports folder is deleted after reports.retentionDays.
  • Nothing is deleted. Archived records stay in the Sheet. Each month, audit rows older than the month before move to a spreadsheet of their own in the Files folder, Six7 Contracts & Providers — Audit and the year.

6.2 Turning them on or off#

  • The digest: an Admin ticks or unticks Digest for each person, in Admin tools › Users or _Users.
  • The procurement overview: an Admin sets ReportEmail.
  • The approval and decision emails always go: they're how approvals reach people.
  • Admin tools › Send report email now sends the procurement overview at once, to every recipient or to you only, to see what it looks like. Run reminders now runs the whole daily routine: the contract job, the digests, the report emails due and the cleanup.

6.3 Daily quotas#

Every email counts against the owner account's daily limit, set by Google: about 1,500 recipients a day for a Workspace account, which a digest per person and the approval emails fit easily. Check installation shows what's left today.

An email over the limit is skipped, not sent later. The records stay in the app, and the next day's emails go out as usual.

9.4 Security and privacy#

On top of Suite §7:

  • Individuals' privacy: an Individual can't reach records outside their scope through any screen, search, export, report, link or email. A record they can't see is reported as not found, so the app doesn't even confirm it exists.
  • Provider documents are open only to Procurement Managers and Admins, in the app and in Drive.
  • Files: a link pasted into a record that points to the Files folder works only for a file the person can already open. To attach someone else's file, upload a copy.
  • Who changed what: the _Audit tab records every change. Contracts, providers and purchase requests show theirs with History; a provider's only to its owner, Procurement Managers and Admins.
  • Text stays text: a title that starts with = is stored as typed, never as a formula.
  • Emails stay inside: people are added only with an address in your company's domain, so digests and reports never leave it.

9.5 Stop, park or remove the app#

Contracts & Providers stops from the Danger zone, at the bottom of the Sheet's Six7 menu: Park, Resume, Retire (keep the Sheet) and Wipe. Only the owner account can use them; anyone else gets "Only the owner account can run Park."

In this version you can Park and Resume. Retire and Wipe come with the next version; until then they answer: "Retire comes with the next version of Six7 Contracts & Providers. To stop the app now, use Six7 › Danger zone › Park: it keeps everything, and Resume brings it back."

Park stops Contracts & Providers until you resume it, and keeps everything:

  • it deletes its six triggers: Nightly, Cleanup, Daily job, Digest and report emails, On edit and On change;
  • its address answers one line to everyone: "Six7 Contracts & Providers is parked. Ask the owner, the owner account's address, to resume it.";
  • no email, digest or report goes out, and nothing runs in the background, the daily job's renewal reviews, roll-overs and expiries included;
  • the Sheet, the Files folder and every report stay as they are.
  1. As the owner account, open the Sheet and click Six7 › Danger zone › Park.
  2. Read what will happen, type the Sheet's name to confirm, and click OK. Capitals, spaces and the kind of dash don't count: a plain - works for the — in Six7 Contracts & Providers — your company. With any other name, nothing changes: "That isn't the Sheet's name, so nothing was changed."
  3. The summary ends with "Parked (version 0.1.0)." The owner account also gets it by email, "Six7 Contracts & Providers was parked", and _Audit records a PARK row. If a step failed, the last line adds "Fix the lines marked ✗, then run Check installation again."

If the daily job is half way through and waiting to go on when you park, Park's first screen and its summary say so: "The daily job is half way and waiting to go on: it stops, and Resume doesn't restart it. It runs again at 05:00, or at once with Six7 › Run reminders now."

While it's parked:

  • Everyone who opens the address sees only the parked line, Admins too, so Admin tools can't be reached. A page that was already open shows the same line the next time it asks the app for something.
  • Setup / repair still repairs the install, so Six7 can deliver an update to a parked app, but it installs no trigger: "⚠ Parked since … by …: no trigger was installed. Six7 › Danger zone › Resume brings it back."
  • Check installation starts with "⚠ State: Parked since …" and shows "✓ Triggers: none, as a parked install should have". If a trigger was left behind, it names it, and Park again removes it.
  • Validate data starts with "Parked since … by …".
  • In the Sheet's Six7 menu, Run reminders now and Send report email now don't run: "Run reminders now doesn't run: Six7 Contracts & Providers is parked. Six7 › Danger zone › Resume brings it back." The rest of the menu still works for the owner account: Open app, Setup / repair, Validate data, Import pasted rows and Check installation.

Resume brings Contracts & Providers back, at the same address:

  1. As the owner account: Six7 › Danger zone › Resume, and type the Sheet's name.
  2. It installs the six triggers again, with the digest at digest.hour, and refreshes the app's copies of the data tabs, so anything typed into the Sheet while parked shows at once. The address opens the app again for everyone with access, and emails, the digest, the renewal reviews and reports go out again from their next run.
  3. The summary ends with "Resumed (version 0.1.0)." The owner account gets "Six7 Contracts & Providers was resumed", with an Open Six7 Contracts & Providers button, and _Audit records a RESUME row.

If Park stopped the daily job half way, Resume doesn't restart it, and its summary says so. Run Six7 › Run reminders now to finish it the same day; otherwise it runs again at 05:00.

Closing the address is optional, and only by hand. Park keeps the address, so anyone who opens it reads the parked line. To close it: open the Sheet › Extensions › Apps Script › Deploy › Manage deployments, choose the web app, and Archive it. Opening the app again then needs a new address: after Resume, make a new deployment, clear app.url in _Settings, and open the new address once as the owner account. Six7 can do it with you in a delivery window. Old bookmarks and links in past emails keep pointing at the closed address.

I trashed the Sheet. Has the app stopped? No. Its address still opens the app, and its triggers are still installed. Restore the Sheet from Drive's trash, Park it and close its address, then trash it again if you want to. Or, without restoring it, delete its triggers as the owner account at https://script.google.com/home/triggers: ⋮ › Delete trigger on each one. That stops its jobs, but its address keeps opening the app until you restore the Sheet and close the address. The triggers page names each row by its project only, and two installs can share a name, so check ⋮ › Project details before you delete.

What you seeWhat to do
"Sign in with your work account"The app can't tell who the person is. See Suite §5
"You don't have access to Six7 Contracts & Providers yet"access.unlisted is None and the person isn't in _Users, or their Active box is unticked (§4.3)
"Six7 Contracts & Providers is parked. Ask the owner, …, to resume it."The owner account parked the app. Only the owner account can resume it: Six7 › Danger zone › Resume (§9.5)
"Only the owner account (…) can run setup"Someone else ran Setup / repair. Sign in as the owner account
"No approver is available for this request"The requester is the only member of the group the rules chose. Add a second person to that group (§3.4)
A request or renewal went to the wrong groupOpen it and read "Routed to … by rule …": that rule matched first. Check the rules' order and conditions with Test a scenario (§3.5). Decided items keep their rule
A contract has no renewal reviewIt needs status Active and an end date. The review opens when the daily job reaches the end date minus the rule's lead time, or earlier for a long notice period (§3.4). A cycle whose end date has passed gets none
A contract renewed without a decisionIts review was still pending at the end date, so it lapsed. Decide renewals before the notice deadline
"Renewed: notice not recorded"Not renewing was decided, but nobody recorded the notice before the end date, so the contract rolled over for another term. If the notice was in fact sent, correct the contract with Edit: its end date and its status
People receive "shared with you" emailsSomething in the install has changed. Run Check installation, then send Six7 its lines
Dates are one day offThe Sheet's and the script's time zones differ. Run Setup / repair, which aligns them, then Check installation
No digest emailsCheck Digest in _Users, then try Run reminders now
No report emailOnly Procurement Managers and Admins get it, as ReportEmail says; weekly ones come on Mondays
"The app is busy, please try again"Many changes arrived at once. Try again. If it happens often in normal use, tell Six7
An upload failsThe file is over 10 MB: upload a smaller one or paste a link. If it still fails, run Setup / repair
Someone's report copy is missingThe copy is made when they click Make a copy on Google's page. Otherwise they can generate the report again
Data disappeared after a column was renamedThe app finds columns by their header. Rename it back, or run Setup / repair to add the column again and move the data into it
The tab shows Google's iconRun Setup / repair. If your company doesn't allow sharing with everyone in it, the icon stays (§1.4)
