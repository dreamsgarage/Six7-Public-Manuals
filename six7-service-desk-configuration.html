1. Before you start#

1.1 What the Service Desk does for you#

Six7 Service Desk turns every request your company receives into a ticket with an owner and a due time, within minutes, without anyone watching a shared inbox. Employees keep emailing it@ or hr@ as they do today; customers keep emailing help@. Each email becomes a ticket, the requester gets an acknowledgement with a reference and a time, and the replies go back from the same address, in the same email thread.

  • Two audiences: Internal (your employees) and External (customers and anyone outside the company).
  • Queues answer them: IT, HR and Facilities for employees and Help for customers to start with. You rename, add or remove queues.
  • Rules give every ticket an owner, measure the service levels you set, and escalate when a target is at risk.

People work in one app. The desk's screens are My queue, Tickets, Board, Resources and Reports, plus Admin tools for those who may use them. Employees asking for help have My requests and New request. The Service Desk User manual shows each screen.

1.3 The desk's owner account#

The Service Desk runs as an account of its own in your Workspace, for example six7-desk@yourcompany.com (Suite §5):

  • Its mailbox is the desk inbox. The app reads it every few minutes and sends the desk's emails from it. Nobody else uses it for mail, and nobody works in it by hand.
  • It needs a Google Workspace licence (Business Starter or above), with Gmail and Apps Script turned on, and 2-Step Verification.
  • It owns the forms, the Files folder and the Knowledge folder.
  • Google's daily limits, such as how many emails it can send, are its own (§6.3).

Important

Keep the owner's mailbox for the desk. Every new email in it is read by the desk. Anything not sent to one of the desk's addresses is set aside for the Admins in the Intake log. A second Six7 install under the same owner account must not read the same inbox (intake.readMailbox, §3.2).

1.4 What the Workspace admin needs to do#

The Service Desk needs a few settings only your Google Workspace admin can make:

  1. The queue addresses. In the Admin console, add each queue address as an alias of the owner account: Directory › Users › the owner › User information › Alternate email addresses. Wait a few minutes before testing.
  2. Sending as the queues. In the owner's Gmail, Settings › Accounts › Send mail as › Add another email address, once per alias, with the queue's name as the sender name (for example IT or Help), Treat as an alias ticked. Google verifies an alias of the same account at once.
  3. A queue that keeps a Google Group address instead of an alias: the group must forward every message to the owner account, with no subject prefix and no footer, with replies going to the author, and the group's address added to the owner's Send mail as too.
  4. Let Six7 deliver the app: API controls may need to trust clasp, the Google tool Six7 uses during a delivery window (Suite §8).
  5. Optional:
    • allow incoming webhooks in Google Chat, for the queues' Chat spaces (§3.13);
    • allow forms that people outside the company can answer, for the customers' contact form (§3.10);
    • make the app easy to find: a short address such as desk.yourcompany.com, a tile in the Google apps menu, or a managed bookmark. Six7 provides the icons.

Your domain's SPF, DKIM and DMARC records should already cover Gmail sending as these addresses. If replies from the desk land in customers' spam, check them first.

What this release fixes:

  • Seven statuses: New, Open, Waiting on requester, Waiting on others, Resolved, Closed and Cancelled.
  • The first response is the first public reply by a person. The acknowledgement and the status emails (asking the requester for something, the resolution) don't count.
  • Three escalation steps. You choose when the first and the last happen, and who gets the last; a breach always tells the queue's leads (else the audience's Managers, else the Admins).
  • One mailbox per desk: every queue address delivers to the owner account's inbox, as an alias or a forwarding Google Group.
  • Requesters aren't listed and need no seat. People outside the company use email or the contact form, never the app.
  • Rules apply to new emails only; replies join their ticket.
  • No approvals, custom fields, merged tickets, customer portal, or automation beyond routing and recurring tickets.
  • Requests arrive by email, forms and the app. There is no chat, WhatsApp or phone intake; agents log a phone call with New ticket.
  • English only.

Three questions to ask of your current process:

  1. Who owns a request in its first minute? Inside the queue's hours the desk always names someone: the owner, or the dispatchers or leads who will give it one. Outside them, unless someone is on call, it waits for the hours.
  2. Which requests are confidential, and who may read them? Here it's decided per queue, with a flag on each ticket.
  3. What happens out of hours, and when a reply is late? Here the clocks run in business hours (or around the clock, for a 24x7 level), an on-call person gets every new ticket outside the hours, and three escalation steps tell the right people.

2. First sign-in and setup#

2.1 Run Setup / repair#

Six7 installs the app. Then the owner account finishes it:

  1. Open the Sheet as the owner account.
  2. Click Six7 › Setup / repair.
  3. Google asks the owner account to allow Gmail, Drive and Forms. Click Advanced, then **Go to Six7 Service Desk — your company** (it says unsafe, because the app is your own private copy and isn't published by Google), tick Select all, then Continue. A run with a permission left unticked stops and asks again.
  4. Reload the Sheet when it's done. It takes under a minute.

Setup / repair creates the tabs, the default queues IT, HR, Facilities and Help with their categories, the Standard service level for both audiences, the Files folder and the triggers, and adds the owner account as the first Admin. Only the owner account can run it. It's safe to run again: it only adds what's missing.

2.2 Read the setup summary#

Setup / repair ends with a summary: one line per change, marked ✓, or ✗ for a step that couldn't run, then the Check installation lines (§2.3), and "Setup complete (version 0.1.0)." last. When a line shows ✗, the last line adds "Fix the lines marked ✗, then run Check installation again."

On a new install, Desk addresses shows ⚠ "none yet" until you set the queue addresses (§3.5). If a line shows ✗ after a second run, send Six7 a screenshot (Suite §10).

3.4 Plan your queues#

Plan the desk on paper before typing into the tabs. The tabs are quick to fill once you know who asks, at which address, and who answers; most setup mistakes come from skipping that step.

Answer these first:

  1. Who asks? Your employees (Internal), your customers (External), and any systems that send alerts.
  2. Which addresses do people already use? For example it@, hr@ and help@, or one shared address for everything.
  3. Which teams answer, and who leads each one? Each team becomes a queue.
  4. What is confidential? Payroll, health and similar matters belong in a queue that starts confidential.
  5. Who hands out new tickets? Nobody (the rule does), one dispatcher during the day, or always a lead.
  6. Who covers nights and weekends, if anyone? And which customers have contractual targets?

The model. Each audience has queues, and each queue has categories. Addresses lead into queues: a queue's own address, or a general address whose rules choose the queue. People join queues.

There is no separate group to create: the queue is the group. Its members are the people who list it in their Queues on the Users screen. Its leads are named in _Queues, or, when none are named, they're its members with the Queue Lead role. It answers from its own address, or, without one, from its audience's general address. A Google Group can still be the address people write to, as long as it forwards every message to the desk's owner account and is one of its Send mail as addresses (§1.4).

Three common layouts:

LayoutHow it looksGood for
One queue per teamEmployees write to it@, hr@ or facilities@; each address is one Internal queue, and HR starts confidentialMost companies: people already know which team to write to
Customer support as wellAdd help@ as an External queue, Help. A key customer can have a service level of its own (§3.8, §3.9)Companies that answer customers from a shared inbox today
One address for everythingEmployees write to requests@, set as general.Internal. The queues keep no address of their own, and rules choose the queue: vpn|wifi|network to IT, payslip|payroll to HR, anything else to routing.defaultInternalCompanies where people don't know, or shouldn't need to know, which team handles what

Auto, Hold or Manual?

ModeChoose it whenWatch out for
Auto (the default)Any member can take any ticket, and speed mattersThe rule follows availability: Away, hours and limits must be kept up to date
HoldOne person sorts the new tickets during the dayDispatchers must also be members of the queue, and only leads can assign: other dispatchers take. A hold happens only inside the queue's hours, when a dispatcher is available, and lasts 15 minutes by default (assign.holdMinutes)
ManualEvery ticket needs a lead's judgement, as in HRLeads are notified of each new ticket. A Manual queue with no leads assigns automatically

Traps to avoid:

  • A dispatcher who isn't a member of the queue starts the hold but can't see the ticket.
  • A routing rule that names a queue moves every email it matches, from every address of its audience (§3.7).
  • A category belongs to one queue. A rule that names another queue's category falls back to the queue's default category.
  • A 24x7 target needs an on-call person in every queue that uses it.
  • Keep at least one active queue per audience, and keep the default queues (routing.defaultInternal, routing.defaultExternal) active.
  • A queue with no active Agent or Queue Lead can't give tickets an owner: Managers and Admins who list it don't count. Check installation's Ladder check shows it.

How a new ticket gets its owner:

  • Auto (the default): the rule assigns it at once to the first available agent of the queue.
  • Hold: the queue's dispatchers (the Dispatchers and the leads) see it under To dispatch in My queue and get a notice. They have assign.holdMinutes (15) to take it, or, as Queue Leads, to assign it; then the rule does. The hold only happens inside the queue's hours while a dispatcher is available, and a Hold queue with no dispatchers and no leads assigns automatically. Make every dispatcher a member of the queue: one who isn't still starts the hold, but can't see the ticket.
  • Manual: the leads assign every ticket by hand, and each new one is notified to them (at once, or in their digest, as their Notify says). A Manual queue with no leads assigns automatically.

The rule picks, among the queue's Agents and Queue Leads who are available now (active, not Away, inside the queue's hours, or on call) and under their capacity: someone with the category's skill if there is one; for customers, the agent who last helped that requester; then whoever has the fewest open tickets; then whoever waited longest since their last ticket. The ticket shows why, for example "assigned by rule: fewest open (2); skill: network".

When nobody is available inside hours, the ticket waits under Unassigned and the leads are told at once. Outside hours it waits for the first person whose hours start, unless the queue has an on-call person.

3.7 Routing rules#

Rules in _Routing decide the queue, category and priority of new email tickets. They're tried in Order, and the first rule that matches decides. Without a match, an email to a queue's address goes to that queue, and one to a general address goes to its audience's default queue; what a rule leaves blank falls back to the queue's defaults. Setup creates none.

Important

A rule that names a queue moves every email it matches, whatever address it was sent to: from any address of its Audience, or of both audiences when Audience is blank. Give every rule an Audience, and keep keyword rules narrow: a rule on the word network also moves an email to hr@ that mentions it.

3.10 Forms#

Admin tools › Create forms (Admins) builds three Google Forms owned by the owner account:

  • Employees' request form: for people in your company only. They choose the queue and category, describe the request, and can copy colleagues.
  • Customers' contact form: anyone with the link can answer it, without signing in. Put its link on your website's contact page. The acknowledgement carries it too.
  • Satisfaction form: opened from the Yes and No links of the resolution email.

Their links show in Admin tools and never change. Responses become tickets on the next intake run. Run Create forms again after changing queues, categories or the public Resources items, so the forms list them.

3.11 Resources and canned replies#

The Knowledge folder (in the Files folder) holds the Docs your team links to. The Resources screen holds three kinds of items, per queue and category:

  • Replies: canned replies, inserted into an answer with {{requester}} (the requester's first name), {{ticket}}, {{title}}, {{agent}} and {{queue}} filled in;
  • Links: a Doc in the Knowledge folder, a Drive file or a web address;
  • Snippets: a short answer shown as text.

Queue Leads add and edit the items of the queues they lead, and Admins all of them. A link or snippet can be Public: requesters see it on New request and the forms, and in the acknowledgement's "Before you wait" line. Items of queues that start confidential are never public.

3.13 Google Chat#

A queue can post to its team's Google Chat space: tickets to dispatch or unassigned, due soon, breached, overdue, and intake stopping.

  1. In the Chat space, Apps & integrations › Webhooks › Add webhook, named Six7 Service Desk.
  2. Paste its address into the queue's ChatWebhook in _Queues.

Chat is for the team only. Messages about confidential tickets carry only the number, the queue and the priority.

4.4 People you didn't add#

The access.unlisted setting decides what happens when someone in your company opens the app without a row in _Users:

  • Requester (the default): they can ask for help with New request and follow their requests in My requests.
  • None: they see "You don't have access to Six7 Service Desk yet", with the queues' addresses to email instead and the Admins to ask.

People outside your company's domain never get in. Personal domains such as gmail.com never count as your company, even if someone with such an address is listed.

4.5 Scope and visibility#

  • Agents and Queue Leads see the tickets of their queues, in either audience.
  • Managers see every ticket of their audiences, including the non-confidential tickets of queues that start confidential.
  • Confidential tickets are seen only by the members of their queue, that queue's Managers (the Managers who list it in their Queues) and Admins. A ticket starts confidential when its queue has ConfidentialByDefault; an agent of the queue can set or clear the flag.
  • Files on internal notes open only for the queue's members, its Managers and Admins.
  • Transferring a confidential ticket shows its whole conversation to the new queue. The transfer dialog says so and offers Hand over as a linked ticket instead: a new ticket with only what the other queue needs.

Example: Valeria emails hr@ about her payslip. The ticket starts confidential. Laura, who leads HR, sees it; Carolina, the Internal Manager, doesn't, unless HR is in her Queues; Sofía in IT never does. If Laura needs IT to fix Valeria's laptop access, she hands over a linked ticket to IT with just that.

4.6 Backup Admin and Sheet editors#

Important

The script runs as the owner, so anyone who can edit the Sheet or its script can read every ticket, confidential ones included. Only the owner account and one backup Admin may edit the Sheet; everyone else uses the app. Check installation's Sheet editors line lists everyone besides the owner, the backup Admin included: check that nobody else is there. Never share the Files folder or its subfolders either: the app gives each file to exactly the people allowed to see its ticket.

Give a second person the Admin role and edit access to the Sheet, so someone can stand in if the owner account is unavailable.

5. Your data#

5.1 Start empty, with demo data, or with your own#

  • Empty: the usual start. Requests arrive by email from day 1.
  • Your existing tracker's open tickets and your customers: §5.2.
  • Demo data: thirteen weeks of a fictional company's desk, for trying the Service Desk out. Six7 loads it only into a demo install, never into the one your company will use, because the app has no step to remove it.

5.2 Import from a spreadsheet#

Admins import, once, from an earlier tracker:

  1. Check File › Settings › Locale in the Sheet, so the dates you paste are read the right way.
  2. Paste your customers into _Organisations first: Domain, Name, ServiceLevel, Contract.
  3. Paste tickets into Tickets, below the header, with ID and the system columns blank: Title, Audience, Queue, Category, Priority, Status, RequesterEmail, Organisation, ReceivedAt, Assignee. Use the exact values of the lists.
  4. Run Validate data, fix what it lists, then Admin tools › Import pasted rows.

Rows become tickets where they lie. A ticket with an assignee keeps them; an open one without goes through the rule. Nobody is emailed, not even the assignees. A row with a problem is skipped and listed with the reason, and left as pasted: fix it and import again.

5.3 Export and backups#

  • Google keeps the Sheet's history. File › Version history shows earlier versions. Name a version before big changes.
  • A copy for safekeeping: File › Make a copy saves a snapshot. Never ask people to use a copy: it has no app, no intake and no triggers.
  • Exports from the app: Tickets › Export to Sheets exports the list as filtered, every page; every report exports to Sheets, a Doc or a PDF. Sheets and Docs open on Google's Make a copy page, which saves them in the person's own Drive; PDFs download.
  • Nothing is deleted. Tickets stay forever. Each month the audit, and the messages of tickets closed more than 90 days ago, move to a yearly archive spreadsheet; the ticket says where. The Intake log keeps 30 days.

9.4 Security and privacy#

On top of Suite §7:

  • Confidential tickets stay private in every screen, search, report, export, email and Chat message (§4.5).
  • Files live in the Files folder, which is never shared. Opening a file gives that one person view access, without an email.
  • Guards on what comes in: the spam folder is never read; outside senders and domains are limited per hour; blocked senders, auto-replies, bounces and mail loops never open tickets; programs (.exe, .js, .msi and the like) attached to emails aren't saved.
  • Who changed what: the _Audit tab records every change, including every assignment and the rule or person behind it. Each ticket shows its History.

9.5 Stop, park or remove the desk#

The desk stops from the Danger zone, at the bottom of the Sheet's Six7 menu: Park, Resume, Retire (keep the Sheet) and Wipe. Only the owner account can use them; anyone else gets "Only the owner account can run Park."

In this version you can Park and Resume. Retire and Wipe come with the next version; until then they answer: "Retire comes with the next version of Six7 Service Desk. To stop the app now, use Six7 › Danger zone › Park: it keeps everything, and Resume brings it back."

Park stops the desk until you resume it, and keeps everything:

  • it deletes its six triggers: Intake, Nightly, Cleanup, Daily job, Digest and report emails, and Sheet edits;
  • its address answers one line to everyone: "Six7 Service Desk is parked. Ask the owner, the owner account's address, to resume it.";
  • nothing runs in the background: no email becomes a ticket, nothing is assigned or escalated, and no notice, digest or report goes out;
  • email to the desk waits in the mailbox, and what people send through the request forms waits in the forms, which stay open: the first intake run after Resume reads both;
  • the Sheet, the Files folder, the forms, the Gmail labels and every report stay as they are.

After a park of more than 14 days, mail older than that may need handling by hand; Park's first screen says so too.

  1. As the owner account, open the Sheet and click Six7 › Danger zone › Park.
  2. Read what will happen, type the Sheet's name to confirm, and click OK. Capitals, spaces and the kind of dash don't count: a plain - works for the — in Six7 Service Desk — your company. With any other name, nothing changes: "That isn't the Sheet's name, so nothing was changed."
  3. The summary ends with "Parked (version 0.1.0)." The owner account also gets it by email, "Six7 Service Desk was parked", and _Audit records a PARK row. If a step failed, the last line adds "Fix the lines marked ✗, then run Check installation again."

If an intake run is reading the mailbox at that moment, Park changes nothing and says: "An intake run is reading the mailbox now, so nothing was changed. Park when it ends, in a minute or two."

While it's parked:

  • Everyone who opens the address sees only the parked line, Admins too, so Admin tools can't be reached. A page that was already open shows the same line the next time it asks the desk for something. The Six7 Workspace add-on, when you have it, gets the same answer.
  • Setup / repair still repairs the desk, so Six7 can deliver an update to a parked desk, but it installs no trigger: "⚠ Parked since … by …: no trigger was installed. Six7 › Danger zone › Resume brings it back."
  • Check installation starts with "⚠ State: Parked since …" and shows "✓ Triggers: none, as a parked desk should have". If a trigger was left behind, it names it, and Park again removes it.
  • Validate data starts with "Parked since … by …".
  • In the Sheet's Six7 menu, Check mailbox now, Run reminders now, Send report email now and Check delivery don't run: "Check mailbox now doesn't run: Six7 Service Desk is parked. Six7 › Danger zone › Resume brings it back." The rest of the menu still works for the owner account: Open app, Setup / repair, Create forms, Validate data, Import pasted rows and Check installation.

Resume brings the desk back, at the same address:

  1. As the owner account: Six7 › Danger zone › Resume, and type the Sheet's name.
  2. It installs the six triggers again, intake every intake.everyMinutes minutes and the digest at digest.hour, and clears the app's cached copy of the configuration, so what was typed into the Sheet while parked counts at once. The address opens the app again for everyone with access.
  3. The first intake run, within intake.everyMinutes minutes, reads the email and the form responses that came while it was parked and makes their tickets. Notices, the digest and reports go out again from their next run. The time it was parked isn't reported to the Admins as intake having stopped.
  4. The summary ends with "Resumed (version 0.1.0)." The owner account gets "Six7 Service Desk was resumed", with an Open Six7 Service Desk button, and _Audit records a RESUME row.

Closing the address is optional, and only by hand. Park keeps the address, so anyone who opens it reads the parked line. To close it: open the Sheet › Extensions › Apps Script › Deploy › Manage deployments, choose the web app, and Archive it. Opening the desk again then needs a new address: after Resume, make a new deployment, clear app.url in _Settings, and open the new address once as the owner account. Six7 can do it with you in a delivery window. Old bookmarks and links in past emails keep pointing at the closed address.

I trashed the Sheet. Has the desk stopped? No. Its address still opens the app, and its triggers are still installed. Restore the Sheet from Drive's trash, Park it and close its address, then trash it again if you want to. Or, without restoring it, delete its triggers as the owner account at https://script.google.com/home/triggers: ⋮ › Delete trigger on each one. That stops its jobs, but its address keeps opening the app until you restore the Sheet and close the address. The triggers page names each row by its project only, and two installs can share a name, so check ⋮ › Project details before you delete.
