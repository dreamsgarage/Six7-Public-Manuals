1. Why Six7#

Google Workspace gives a company email, documents and spreadsheets. It doesn't give it a CRM, a service desk, a contract register or a shared work board. Most small companies fill the gap with spreadsheets and email, or pay for one outside tool per job, priced per person.

Six7 adds those business apps inside the Google account the company already has:

  • Your account, your data. Each app runs in your own Google account. Its records sit in a Google Sheet that you own. Six7 runs no servers and hosts nothing.
  • One price for the whole suite. Six7 is paid as a flat yearly retainer per company, never per person. It covers every Six7 app, so adding an app doesn't change it; its tier follows the size of your company.
  • Your own private copy. The apps aren't sold through the Google Workspace Marketplace. Each company gets its own copy, installed for it and used only by its own people.
  • Simple to use. One link per app, one screen per job, and the basics need no training.

3. How a Six7 app is built#

Every Six7 app has three parts, and all three live in your Google account:

  1. The Sheet holds the data. Each kind of record has a tab, and the settings, lists and users have tabs too.
  2. The script is the app's logic. It's attached to the Sheet.
  3. The web app is the screen people use. It opens from one link in the browser, on a computer or a phone.

People work in the web app, not in the Sheet. The app checks each person's role before it shows or changes anything. Someone who edits the Sheet directly skips those checks, which is why only the Owner account and a backup Admin edit it (§7).

Everything runs on the Google subscription you already pay for, plus, in a Team, a Workspace licence for each Owner account (§5). There's nothing extra to host, and nothing runs on Six7's computers.

The app's footer shows its version. Have it ready when you ask for help (§10).

5. Accounts and signing in#

The Owner account Solo#

Your apps are installed in your own @gmail.com account, so it's the Owner account, and you're the Owner, the Admin and the only user at once. There's no second account.

That account holds everything: the apps, their Sheets and your data. Keep it safe:

  • turn on 2-Step Verification;
  • don't share its password with anyone, Six7 included;
  • keep its recovery email and phone number up to date.

The Owner account Team#

Your apps are installed in a dedicated Owner account in your Workspace, for example six7-admin@yourcompany.com. It's used only for Six7 and works like a service account: nobody works from it day to day.

The Service Desk always gets an Owner account of its own, such as six7-desk@yourcompany.com, and so does Kanban when you also use another Six7 app. The CRM and Contracts & Providers can share one. Google's daily limits belong to each account, so the busiest apps don't share them.

  • Your company creates each one and keeps its password. Six7 never asks for it.
  • Each one needs a Google Workspace licence, as any user does.
  • Turn on 2-Step Verification for it.
  • Decide who holds it, usually your Workspace admin, and who stands in for them.
  • The apps run as this account. That's why people can use an app without having access to its Sheet.
  • Only this account can run the tools of the Sheet's Six7 menu, such as Setup / repair and Check installation. Your Admins use Admin tools in each app.

Everyone else, your Admins and Members, signs in with their own work account.

Signing in#

Open the app's link in your browser. Google signs you in. There's no separate Six7 password.

  1. Bookmark the link. Open it in the browser where you're signed in to the right Google account.
  2. If you use more than one Google account, for example a personal and a work one, open the app in a browser profile that has only the right account, or sign out of the others. Otherwise the app may pick the wrong account.
  3. On your phone, if a link in an app email asks you to sign in, open it in your browser with the right account instead of inside the Gmail app.

Keep the apps one click away#

Each app opens from its own link. Keep it where you'll find it, in the browser where you're signed in to the right account:

  • Bookmark it. In Chrome, open the app and click the star at the right of the address bar, or press Ctrl+D (⌘+D on a Mac). Ctrl+Shift+B (⌘+Shift+B) shows the bookmarks bar.
  • Install it as an app. In Chrome, open the app, then ⋮ › Cast, save, and share › Install page as app, and click Install. It opens in a window of its own, like a program, and appears with your computer's apps. To keep it in sight:
    • Windows: right-click its icon on the taskbar, then Pin to taskbar;
    • Mac: right-click its icon in the Dock, then Options › Keep in Dock;
    • Chromebook: right-click its icon on the shelf, then Pin.
  • On a phone, open the link in Chrome and choose ⋮ › Add to Home screen, or in Safari on an iPhone, the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.

The app's own icon. Bookmarks and installed apps take the icon the app shows in the browser tab. Team That's the app's own icon. Solo On a personal account the tab shows Google's icon, and so do they. To give an installed app its own icon, right-click the icon in the table below, then Save image as:

6. Roles, in general Team#

In a Team, each app decides what each person can see and do. An Admin gives each person a role: in Admin tools › Users in the CRM, Contracts & Providers and the Service Desk, and in the Sheet's _Users tab in Kanban. The app checks the role on every action, not only by hiding buttons. Besides the Admins, everyone who uses an app is a Member, with one of the app's roles.

Each app has an Admin role, for settings, users and the Admin tools, and its own roles below it:

AppRoles
KanbanAdmin, Manager, Member
CRMAdmin, Sales Manager, Operations Manager, Sales Rep
Service DeskAdmin, Manager, Queue Lead, Agent. People who only ask for help are requesters and need no role
Contracts & ProvidersAdmin, Procurement Manager, Individual

The roles also set a record scope: whether a person sees every record or only their own. "Their own" means something different in each app: the deals they own in the CRM, the boards they belong to in Kanban, the tickets of their queues in the Service Desk, the contracts and requests that are theirs in Contracts & Providers. Each app's Configuration manual has its full role table, in §4.2.

Some things are the same everywhere:

  • The Owner account runs Setup / repair, and is the app's first Admin.
  • People you didn't add get the role set in the app's access.unlisted setting. Each app has its own default: no access in the CRM and in Contracts & Providers; Kanban's Member role; Requester in the Service Desk.
  • When someone leaves, first hand their work over, as the app's Configuration manual §4.3 explains. Then untick their Active box rather than deleting the row, so their records keep their name. They can no longer open the app. In the Service Desk they can still ask for help as a requester, while access.unlisted lets requesters in, as it does by default.

7. Your data and privacy#

Where your data lives. Every record is in your app's Sheet, in your Google account. Files people add go to your own Google Drive. Nothing is copied to Six7. If you stop working with Six7, the apps and the data stay with you.

Stopping an app. Moving an app's Sheet to Drive's trash doesn't stop the app: its address still opens it, and its background jobs stay installed. Each app is getting a Danger zone in its Sheet's Six7 menu, where the Owner account parks the app and resumes it later. A parked app keeps everything: nothing runs on its own and nothing is sent, and the Owner account can still look after it from the Sheet. All four apps have it in this release, each described in its Configuration manual's §9.5. The CRM can also be retired, closed for good with everything it held kept in one folder and a Retirement statement, or wiped, with everything it made moved to Google's trash.

Files the apps add on their own. Each app records every change in its Sheet's _Audit tab. To keep the Sheet fast, on the nights of the 1st to the 7th of each month it moves the changes from before the previous month into a spreadsheet named after the app and the year, such as Six7 Kanban — Audit 2026, in the app's Files folder. The Service Desk also moves there the messages of tickets closed more than 90 days ago. Nothing is deleted. The first file appears at the start of your third calendar month: an app used from October gets one in early December. The files belong to the Owner account and aren't shared. No app needs them to work, so deleting one breaks nothing, but the history goes with it. Keep them; if you do delete one, empty it from Drive's trash too, or the app can keep adding to it there.

Who can see what. People see records through the web app, which applies their role and scope (§6).

Important

The script runs as the Owner account, so anyone who can open the Sheet, even view-only, can read every record, including private ones, and anyone who can edit the Sheet or its script can change them without the app's checks. Share the Sheet only with the Owner account and, in a Team, one backup Admin. Share the app's Files folder with nobody: the apps share each file with the people who need it. Everyone else uses the app. To see who has access, open the Sheet and click Share.

What Six7 can reach. Six7 has no standing access to your account. It reaches your account only during a delivery window that you open for an install or an update, and closes it the same day (§8). Six7 never asks for your password or your 2-Step Verification codes.

Solo

During a delivery window, the access you approve covers your whole personal account, within the permissions listed on Google's screen. That's why Six7 closes each window the same day. Afterwards you can check for yourself at https://myaccount.google.com/connections that the access is gone.

Team

During a delivery window, the access you approve covers only the Owner account you sign in with: its Apps Script projects and its Drive. Your people's own accounts are never touched.

8. Updates and delivery windows#

Six7 installs and updates your apps for you. Each time, you open a short delivery window for Six7. If a window isn't possible, Six7 can guide you through the same job by hand instead.

Before the first window, once per Owner account: sign in as the Owner account and turn on Google Apps Script API at https://script.google.com/home/usersettings. Only the account itself can do this, and without it the install stops. Team If your Admin console restricts third-party apps, your Workspace admin also marks clasp as trusted; Six7 sends the details.

Then, each time:

  1. A Six7 operator sends you a sign-in link.
  2. You approve it on your own device. Open the link and choose the Owner account (Team: the dedicated Owner account, not your own). Sign in as usual, including 2-Step Verification, and click Allow. Google asks you to let clasp, Google's own tool for Apps Script: create and manage the account's Apps Script projects and their deployments; create Drive files and change the ones it created; see the names and details of the account's Drive files, but not what's in them; read the scripts' logs and Google Cloud settings; and see the account's email address and basic profile.
  3. The browser shows "This site can't be reached". That's expected. Copy the whole address from the address bar and send it back to the operator straight away. The address works only once, only for a few minutes, and only together with a key that stays on Six7's computer.
  4. Six7 installs or updates the app.
  5. Six7 closes the window the same day, right after the delivery: it revokes the access at Google and records the window, when it opened and closed, and what was delivered.
  6. You run Six7 › Setup / repair in the app's Sheet, signed in as the Owner account, whenever it suits you: it doesn't need the window.
  7. First install only: open the app's address once as the Owner account. That records the address the app's emails and links use.

The setup summary. Setup / repair shows a summary: one line per step and check, marked ✓ (fine), ⚠ (worth a look) or ✗ (to fix), with "Setup complete (version …)." last. On a first install, the app's address shows ✗ until step 7, and on Solo the browser tab icon's ✗ is expected. If any other line still shows ✗ after a second run, send Six7 a screenshot. Check installation, in the Sheet's Six7 menu or in the app's Admin tools, runs the same checks again at any time.

The consent screen. On a first install, or when an update needs a new permission, Google asks the Owner account to allow the app. Click Review permissions and choose the Owner account. Because the app is your own private copy and isn't published by Google, Google says it hasn't verified it: click Advanced, then Go to the app's name, marked (unsafe). On the list of permissions, tick Select all, then click Continue: the app needs every one.

Going back. If an update causes a problem, ask Six7 to go back to the previous version. It's done like an update. Setup / repair only ever adds tabs, columns, lists and settings to your Sheet, so your data usually stays as it is.

9. AI in Six7#

Where a Six7 app uses AI, it uses the Gemini features that come with your Google account: Gems, Workspace Studio, and Gemini in Gmail and Sheets. What's available depends on your Google plan.

  • Six7 never adds AI keys, outside AI services or a Google Cloud project to your account.
  • Your data isn't sent to any AI service other than the Gemini features of your own Google account.

In this release only the Service Desk has AI helpers. They're optional, and off until Six7 sets them up with you; its Configuration manual's §7 describes them. The other apps have none.

10. Getting help#

Support is part of your Six7 retainer. Contact Six7 through the channel named in your agreement.

To get an answer quickly, send:

  • the app, for example "Kanban";
  • its version, from the app's footer;
  • what you did and what you expected;
  • a screenshot of the screen or the error;
  • for setup problems, the setup summary from Six7 › Setup / repair.

Team

People in your company ask your app's Admin first. The Admin contacts Six7 when it's something they can't fix.

11. Choosing your language#

The manuals and apps are in English today. More languages will follow. When a manual has more than one language, a Language menu appears at its top. The manual remembers your choice on this device.
